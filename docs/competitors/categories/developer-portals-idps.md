# Developer Portals & IDPs

> Category index. Full research lives in each canonical capture — see [`../metadata-contract.md`](../metadata-contract.md) for why this page carries rows, not research.

## Category boundary

Developer-facing catalogs, scaffolding, self-service workflows, scorecards, and platform interfaces that can front or partially substitute HoBo.

## Why HoBo competes here

A portal can absorb the surface developers touch. HoBo must be the control plane a portal calls, not a thing a portal replaces.

| [**OpenChoreo**](../openchoreo.md) | open-source Kubernetes IDP with an explicit developer-intent abstraction layer and a cell-based runtime boundary | direct | `app-graph`, `environments`, `typed-contracts`, `delivery`, `governance`, `wiring` | Open source, CNCF Sandbox, self-hosted, with Cells that make isolation, traffic policy and observability boundaries enforced at runtime rather than merely described. | OpenChoreo abstracts over Kubernetes; HoBo abstracts over providers, of which Kubernetes is one — the advantage exists only for heterogeneous estates, and HoBo should concede that. | displace, borrow, benchmark | P1 |
| [**Cycloid**](../cycloid.md) | self-service portal over templated IaC whose pre-provision cost estimation and auto-generated topology view are the surfaces HoBo most visibly lacks | overlapping | `authoring`, `environments`, `finops`, `observability`, `governance`, `app-graph` | Cost visible while the choice is being made (open-source TerraCost inside StackForms) and topology diagrams generated from Terraform source so they cannot go stale. | Cycloid has no application model and decides no placement: a form cannot express a dependency graph, and governance by dropdown cannot express a context-dependent rule. | benchmark, borrow, integrate | P2 |

| [**Port**](../port.md) *(primary)* | schema-first developer portal whose Blueprint model is the strongest catalog abstraction in the corpus — a front end HoBo should serve, not fight | complementary | `app-graph`, `governance`, `extensibility`, `agent-surface` | Blueprints let an organization define its own entity types and relations, with auto-discovery keeping the catalog true and a shipped MCP surface over it. | Port models what exists; HoBo decides what should happen. | [**Cortex**](../cortex.md) *(primary)* | catalog-and-scorecards portal whose managed integrations and ownership inference are the operational bar for keeping an estate legible | complementary | `app-graph`, `governance`, `observability` | Vendor-managed integrations and first-class ownership resolution — including inference against an org chart — so the catalog stays true without customer maintenance. | HoBo derives its graph from an authoritative event log rather than reconciling systems that may disagree, and enforces at admission rather than reporting after. | integrate, benchmark, borrow | P2 |
| [**OpsLevel**](../opslevel.md) *(primary)* | portal whose scoped-scorecard model is the sharpest idea in the category: standards proportional to what a service actually is | complementary | `app-graph`, `governance` | Scoped scorecards: standards proportional to a service's maturity and criticality rather than one baseline applied to everything. | HoBo scopes policy by deployment context and has no proportionality axis — a real modelling gap this capture records rather than glosses. | integrate, borrow, benchmark | P2 |
| [**Red Hat Developer Hub**](../red-hat-developer-hub.md) *(primary)* | supported Backstage distribution whose dynamic plugin loading solves the maintenance problem that makes self-hosted Backstage expensive | complementary | `app-graph`, `governance`, `extensibility` | Dynamic, configuration-driven plugin loading removes the fork-build-upgrade treadmill that makes self-hosted Backstage expensive, with enterprise support behind it. | HoBo is the control plane a portal fronts; its wasm component model is a stronger version of the same runtime-loading instinct. | integrate, benchmark, borrow | P3 |
| [**Roadie**](../roadie.md) *(primary)* | hosted Backstage whose exit story — your catalog and templates stay yours — is the cleanest lock-in answer in this cohort | complementary | `app-graph`, `extensibility` | Managed Backstage with the cleanest exit story in the cohort: catalog YAML and templates stay in the customer's repositories in an open format. | Not a control plane at all — Roadie is a portal HoBo should feed, and its portability principle is one HoBo shares and states less clearly. | integrate, benchmark, borrow | P3 |
| [**Atlassian Compass**](../atlassian-compass.md) *(primary)* | ecosystem-distribution portal — a reminder that in this category integration reach and vendor commitment matter more than architecture | complementary | `app-graph`, `governance` | Distribution: for an Atlassian-standardized organization the catalog arrives pre-wired to the issues, docs and repositories people already use. | HoBo keeps the model in the customer's repository; Compass keeps it in Atlassian's format, and retired scaffolding templates in December 2025 with no replacement. | integrate, benchmark | P3 |
