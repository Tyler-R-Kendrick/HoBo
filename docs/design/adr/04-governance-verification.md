# Governance, verification, SLO & cost — consolidated decisions

> Consolidated ADR decisions for **governance, verification, slo & cost**. Each section is one architecture decision; see [`README.md`](./README.md) for the full ADR index.

## ADR-0015
**Contracts & policy are HoBo-native planes; a single `can-deploy`/Gatekeeper gate composes the checks; obligations are durable effects.**

**Decision:** consumer-driven contracts/compatibility ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) and policy decisions ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) are first-class, HoBo-native planes; engines sit behind ports.

1. **Contract plane.** The `contract-engine` runs **PACT-style consumer-driven contract testing as the built-in, default behavior** — an internal implementation detail of the contract plane, never a user-facing option to swap; developers declare contracts and never touch PACT directly. HoBo owns the Contract IR; ecosystems integrate via adapters with no hard dep. **"Verified by construction" is scoped honestly to contract *mode*:** *declared* (WIT/Zod) and *inferred* (compile-time from the wiring graph) contracts are proven by construction; *observed*/*promoted* contracts are **trace-mined from sampled traffic** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) and are therefore **confidence-bounded, not proven** — an unobserved-but-valid producer variant can slip a promotion. So a promoted contract carries the **sample coverage** behind it, the promotion gate records that coverage, and promotion runs inside a **migration window** so a still-unobserved shape surfaces as a `compat.Gate*` failure rather than a silent break.
2. **Policy plane.** The `policy-engine` is a built-in HoBo engine; policy is **typed TypeScript** by default, and HoBo owns the Policy IR.
3. **One Gatekeeper gate composes everything.** Deployability is a single explainable decision — contracts-compatible AND policy-allows/approves AND eval-gates-pass AND conformance AND within-cost AND signed/attested — evaluated at Delta→Set/promote and surfaced as `hobo verify` ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)/[Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)). One event-sourced decision replaces scattered CI gates.
4. **Obligations are durable effects, not advice.** An "allow but require X" obligation (canary/approval/auth/shadow-eval) is realized as a HoBo effect/saga and tracked `ObligationCreated→Satisfied`; an unmet obligation blocks ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)/[Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)).

**Consequences:** compatibility and governance are enforced at a single auditable gate — one event-sourced decision, durable obligations, and HoBo-owned IRs with engines behind ports. Declared/inferred contracts are proven by construction; observed/promoted ones are confidence-bounded (sample-coverage-recorded, migration-windowed). (Resolves ADR-P3.)

## ADR-0044
**Feature flags & progressive delivery.**

### Context
HoBo controls *deploys* with canary/progressive rollout ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)), Delta→Set gates ([Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)), and policy obligations ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)). It also toggles *behavior within a running version* — kill-switches, gradual `%` rollout, per-segment targeting, experiments — without a redeploy. Feature flags are the standard answer (OpenFeature is the CNCF standard for flag evaluation), provided in a way that composes with HoBo's audit, policy, identity, and reactive planes rather than as a bolt-on.

### Decision
1. **A first-class `feature-flag` port (`hobo:feature-flag`), OpenFeature-compatible.** HoBo speaks the OpenFeature evaluation API; the **built-in flag store is the default** (zero-dep, air-gap-friendly), and **OpenFeature providers** (flagd/LaunchDarkly/…) are **opt-in per-Camp adapters** (Matching, [ADR-0033](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0033)). Flag types = boolean/string/number/object. Definitions are **IfC** (declared in the Bindle); values/targeting are runtime state.
2. **Change governance: event-sourced + optionally policy-gated + reactive.** Every value/targeting change is a `flag.*` event ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11), full audit/replay); a flip can pass through the **policy plane** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) so prod kill-switches / risky `%` bumps require approval or carry obligations (e.g. auto-revert on SLO burn); a change is a **`data.Changed`-class signal** so reactive queries/clients (`useFlag`/`useQuery`, [Plan 32](../data-plane.md#plan-32)) re-evaluate live.
3. **In-process, deterministic, identity-aware evaluation.** Components evaluate flags **in-process** against a ruleset synced via the Streamer/event log (edge-friendly, no SPOF); targeting context is the unified **`PrincipalRef`** (`ctx.user` [ADR-0038](02-identity-security.md#adr-0038) / `ctx.identity`); evaluation is a **pure function of `(ruleset@seq, context)`** → replayable + safe inside a reactive `query()` ([ADR-0050](03-data-plane.md#adr-0050)).
   - **A true kill-switch is a fast-path push, not poll-sync.** A normal flip propagates with bounded delay tied to the sync interval, but a flag marked **`killSwitch`** is delivered by **push invalidation over the Streamer** (the reactive-fan-out path, [Plan 32](../data-plane.md#plan-32)) with a **declared end-to-end latency budget** (flip → last in-process evaluator) that is itself an SLO ([ADR-0045](#adr-0045)) — so "kill-switch" names a control whose worst-case latency is bounded and verified, not an unbounded chain of eventually-consistent hops. A `flag.Changed` for a kill-switch that misses the budget raises an alert.
4. **Flags + deploy gates compose** for progressive delivery: canary = the deploy dial, flag `%` = the release dial; health signals can auto-revert a flag via a policy obligation.
5. **Events** `flag.Defined`/`flag.Changed`/`flag.Evaluated`(sampled)/`flag.Archived`; flip-gate decisions ride `policy.Decided`.

### Alternatives considered
- **Flags as policy-plane decisions (no new port).** Rejected: overloads the policy plane with high-frequency runtime evaluations, loses OpenFeature ecosystem compatibility, and conflates "may I" (policy) with "is this on" (flag). (Flags still *use* the policy plane for *gating flips*, which is the right seam.)
- **BYO external flag SDK (no HoBo capability).** Rejected: leaves the listed primitive unowned; flags wouldn't be event-sourced/reactive/gated like the rest of HoBo, and targeting couldn't reuse `ctx.user`/`ctx.identity`.
- **Central evaluation service.** Rejected: per-eval network hop + SPOF + non-deterministic for replay/reactive; in-process sync matches HoBo's grain.
- **Client-side-only evaluation.** Rejected: untrusted, no server enforcement (unusable for kill-switches/entitlements).
- **Deploy-only flag changes.** Rejected: defeats decoupling release from deploy.

### Consequences
- **(+)** Closes #24 with a standard-compatible capability that uniquely composes with HoBo's audit (`flag.*`), policy (gated flips + obligations), identity (`ctx.user` targeting), and reactive (`useFlag` re-eval) planes — more than a standalone flag tool offers.
- **(+)** Built-in default keeps it air-gap-friendly; OpenFeature compatibility avoids lock-in and lets teams bring an existing flag provider.
- **(+)** Deterministic in-process eval is edge-friendly and replay-safe; progressive delivery (deploy + release dials) becomes one coherent story with auto-revert.
- **(−)** A synced ruleset means a bounded propagation delay on an ordinary flip (seconds), like any flag system; **kill-switches** take the push fast path (point 3) with a declared, alerted latency budget rather than the poll-sync interval.
- **(−)** The SLO-burn → auto-revert chain (burn detection → `policy.Decided` → obligation → `flag.Changed`) composes several signals, so for *safety-critical* auto-revert the obligation targets a `killSwitch` flag (fast path) and the chain's end-to-end latency is itself an SLO, not assumed instant.
- **(−)** Another capability + event group to build and conformance-test; the targeting-rule DSL must be kept aligned with the policy/`.allow()` predicate language to avoid a second rule dialect.

## ADR-0045
**SLO-as-code, error-budget gating & alerting model.**

### Context
[Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05) ships built-in SLOs + burn-rate alerts and alert routing as a provider capability; [ADR-0021](08-scope-and-requirements.md#adr-0021) sets NFR/SLO baselines. SLOs are **declarative, app-owned, error-budget-bearing** (the [OpenSLO](../../../standards/openslo.md) model) and the budget feeds HoBo's gates; alerting carries the **grouping / dedup / silencing / inhibition / routing-tree** depth of the [Prometheus AlertManager](../../../technologies/prometheus-alertmanager.md) model. Both live in the observability/reliability area and share the burn-rate signal, so they're decided together.

### Decision
1. **SLOs are declared as code, OpenSLO-aligned.** A Bindle/Camp declares **SLOs + SLIs + objectives + error budgets** (an [OpenSLO](../../../standards/openslo.md)-compatible shape; HoBo speaks the standard, the SLI source is a metric provider behind a port). SLIs are computed from the existing telemetry ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05) OTel/projections). Declarations are versioned config (part of the resolved Set, [Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)). **SRE process SLIs are first-class too:** `sre.mttd` (detect) and `sre.mttr` (resolve) are declared the same way and their burn gates deploys / requires a postmortem ([Plan 40](../operators-sre.md#plan-40)).
2. **Error budgets drive gates.** Burn-rate + remaining-budget is a **policy input**: a policy ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) can **freeze deploys / require approval when the budget is exhausted or burning too fast** (a `can-deploy` dimension, [Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)), and an obligation can **auto-revert a rollout or feature-flag** ([ADR-0044](#adr-0044)) on burn. SLO state is event-sourced (`slo.*`/budget facts derived as a projection). **The freeze must not wedge its own recovery (fix-forward exemption).** A deploy tagged **`incident-remediation`** (the burn-restoring change the SRE operator proposes, [Plan 40](../operators-sre.md#plan-40)) is **exempt from the burn-freeze *dimension* only** — it still must pass contracts + conformance + policy + cost; the burn dimension cannot block the very deploy that would stop the burn. The exemption is itself audited (`policy.Decided` with the remediation tag + the incident it cites), so "fix-forward" is a bounded, attributable bypass, not a blanket override — and `can-deploy` asserts a remediation-tagged deploy is never blocked by the freeze it targets.
3. **Alerting is an AlertManager-style model over HoBo events.** Alerts are derived from SLO burn + health/drift/spend signals ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)/[ADR-0039](#adr-0039)) and routed through a model with **grouping, deduplication, silencing, inhibition, and a routing tree** to **notification receivers** (email/Slack/PagerDuty/webhook) that are **providers behind a port**. Alert lifecycle is **event-sourced** (`alert.Fired`/`alert.Grouped`/`alert.Silenced`/`alert.Inhibited`/`alert.Resolved`) so "why did (or didn't) this page" is replayable — and high-risk policy gates ([Plan 6](../ai-native-agent-auth.md#plan-06)) post to the same receivers.

### Alternatives considered
- **Keep platform-defined SLOs only.** Rejected: apps need to own their own SLOs/budgets, and budgets are most valuable when they *gate* (freeze deploys), which requires them as first-class declared state.
- **Invent a proprietary SLO format.** Rejected: OpenSLO is the standard; speak it (engine/SLI-source behind a port) per HoBo's boundary-standards stance ([ADR-0046](#adr-0046)).
- **Adopt Prometheus AlertManager wholesale as the model.** Rejected as the *model* (kept as an optional provider): HoBo's alerts derive from its **event-sourced** signals, so the routing/silencing/inhibition concepts are adopted but expressed natively + event-sourced; AlertManager (or PagerDuty/etc.) can be a notification-receiver provider.
- **Leave alerting as bare routing.** Rejected: without grouping/dedup/silencing/inhibition, real operations drown in noise.

### Consequences
- **(+)** Closes #25 + #26: SLOs become app-owned, declarative (OpenSLO), and **consequential** (error budgets gate deploys + auto-revert rollouts/flags); alerting gains production-grade grouping/silencing/inhibition, event-sourced for "why did this page" replay.
- **(+)** Reuses existing telemetry ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)), the policy/can-deploy gate ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)/[Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)), feature-flag auto-revert ([ADR-0044](#adr-0044)), and the event log; engines/receivers sit behind ports (OpenSLO tooling, AlertManager/PagerDuty) per the standard stance.
- **(−)** SLI computation fidelity depends on the metric source; cross-Camp SLO aggregation inherits the bounded-staleness model ([ADR-0040](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0040)).
- **(−)** An error-budget *deploy freeze* is a sharp control — its thresholds need tuning to avoid blocking legitimate fixes (mitigations: the **`incident-remediation` fix-forward exemption** above, which keeps the freeze from wedging recovery, plus a break-glass waiver via policy for the rare non-remediation case, [Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)).

## ADR-0046
**Boundary standards: CloudEvents, OCSF & OTLP.**

### Context
HoBo's internal source of truth is its rich `EventEnvelope` ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)) — HoBo-native, carrying `seq`/`streamId`/`causationId`/fencing/idempotency that CloudEvents doesn't. At its **boundaries** — exporting events to external consumers, shipping audit data to a SIEM, emitting telemetry — speaking the relevant open standards avoids lock-in and integration friction. HoBo speaks **OTel/OpenInference** ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)), **CloudEvents** (event export), **OCSF** (security/audit normalization), and **OTLP-native** export.

### Decision
1. **Internal envelope stays native; export is CloudEvents-compatible.** The internal `EventEnvelope` is unchanged (it's a superset). At the **export boundary** (webhooks, external subscribers, the GitOps/Pipelines triggers [Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)), HoBo emits **[CloudEvents](../../../standards/cloudevents.md)** — a deterministic mapping (`type`→`type`, `id`→`id`, `streamId`→`source`+`subject`, `ts`→`time`, `data`→`data`, with HoBo-specific fields as CloudEvents **extensions**). Internal HoBo→HoBo stays native; only the external surface is CloudEvents. **Ordering/causation is preserved only for consumers that read the extensions:** `seq`, `causationId`, and the fencing fields ride as **named CloudEvents extensions**, so a generic CloudEvents consumer that ignores extensions gets at-least-once, *unordered* delivery — **ordered/replayable consumption is an explicit consumer contract** (read the `hobo*` extensions). Conformance asserts a HoBo→CloudEvents→HoBo round-trip preserves `seq`/`causationId` so the documented ordered path actually holds.
2. **Audit log exports to OCSF.** Security-relevant events (`token.*`, `identity.*`, `policy.Decided`, `domain.Cert*`, `registry.BundleImported`, `flag.Changed`, …) map to **[OCSF](../../../standards/ocsf.md)** classes (Authentication, Authorization/Entitlement, Configuration/Change, etc.) for export to a SIEM — a projection/exporter over the existing audit streams ([Plan 12](../security-networking.md#plan-12)), not a change to how HoBo stores them. **Export is a one-way boundary the erasure tombstone cannot reach** ([ADR-0060](#adr-0060) §3): once a PII-bearing event is shipped to an external SIEM, destroying HoBo's per-subject key no longer reaches that copy. So a **PII-bearing field is exported only as crypto-shred-keyed ciphertext (the SIEM holds the wrapped form) or is redacted/excluded from the export** — the export carries no plaintext that survives an erasure. The SIEM's own retention is documented as an out-of-scope downstream HoBo cannot erase.
3. **OTLP-native telemetry, confirmed.** Telemetry (metrics/traces/logs incl. OpenInference AI spans) exports over **OTLP** to the tenant's backend ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)) — already the design; this ADR pins it as the committed wire format so there's no proprietary telemetry path.
4. **All three are boundary mappings behind exporters**, not model changes — HoBo's event-sourced core is untouched; the standards are how it *speaks to the outside*.

### Alternatives considered
- **Make the internal `EventEnvelope` literally CloudEvents.** Rejected: CloudEvents lacks `seq`/optimistic-concurrency/fencing/causation semantics HoBo's replay + audit depend on; map at the boundary instead, keeping the superset internally.
- **Proprietary audit/event/telemetry formats.** Rejected: lock-in + integration friction; speaking CloudEvents/OCSF/OTLP is the boundary-standards stance and a security/compliance selling point.
- **Skip OCSF (just ship raw audit).** Rejected: enterprises run SIEMs that ingest OCSF; a normalized export is high-value and cheap (a projection).

### Consequences
- **(+)** Closes #27: HoBo interoperates at its boundaries (CloudEvents subscribers, OCSF SIEMs, OTLP backends) without a proprietary format, while keeping its richer internal model.
- **(+)** All three are **exporters/projections** over the existing log — minimal new machinery, no core change, and they compose with the existing event catalog ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)).
- **(+)** OCSF audit export strengthens the enterprise/regulated story alongside air-gap ([ADR-0036](07-portability-extensibility.md#adr-0036)) and the credential audit ([Plan 6](../ai-native-agent-auth.md#plan-06)).
- **(−)** Boundary mappings must be maintained as the standards evolve (CloudEvents/OCSF versioning) and as HoBo's event catalog grows — an ongoing mapping-fidelity cost.

## ADR-0047
**Chaos / continuous-resilience verification tier.**

### Context
HoBo lists fault-injection tests (kill the Yard mid-deploy, partition an actor, inject provider failures, [Plan 9](../verification.md#plan-09)) and defines a failure taxonomy + reliability algorithms ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)). On top of those sits the **discipline**: a steady-state-hypothesis-driven, blast-radius-controlled, continuous/automated chaos tier that *validates the resilience claims* ([ADR-0021](08-scope-and-requirements.md#adr-0021) RTO/RPO; canary auto-rollback; saga compensation) rather than asserting them. HoBo is unusually well-suited to this because **event-sourcing + deterministic replay make failures cheap to reproduce**.

### Decision
1. **Chaos is a first-class verification tier** ([Plan 9](../verification.md#plan-09)), alongside unit / conformance / integration / E2E / determinism-replay. Each chaos experiment is defined by a **steady-state hypothesis** (an SLO/health invariant, [ADR-0045](#adr-0045)), a **fault to inject**, a **blast-radius bound** (tenant/Camp/resource scope), and an **abort condition**.
2. **Fault injection reuses HoBo's own seams** — kill/partition an actor (lease/fencing, [Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)), drop/delay events, fail a provider call (the Executor boundary), expire a token, trip the spend breaker, revoke a session — driven through a **`chaos` capability** (a test/ops-time injector, policy-gated so it can never run unscoped in prod). External engines (Chaos Mesh / LitmusChaos / AWS FIS) are **optional providers** behind it. `fault` is also a **boundary mode** ([ADR-0076](#adr-0076)): scoping injection to a **sandbox lane** lets a hypothesis be tested **without faulting the real service**. The containment is only real if the lane is **strictly isolated** — `EventEnvelope.lane`-filtered (no fault/poison-event reaches a baseline subscriber, [ADR-0076](#adr-0076) R1) and no **shared baseline lease** held (fork-on-touch, R3); a chaos/self-heal experiment that *can't* be contained that way (e.g. it must hold a real lease) is routed to an **ephemeral-Camp clone**, not a shared-baseline lane (R7) — so faulting a lane never indirectly faults the real service via a shared lock/queue/subscriber. **Containment is decided by a gate, not by trusting an upfront label.** A **pre-flight check at lane admission** (run by the lane controller, the same actor that mints the lane token) **statically analyzes the experiment's declared seams** — does it declare a baseline lease, async egress to a baseline subscriber, or a non-overlayable external side effect? — and a **runtime guard denies the lane at first attempt** to take a baseline single-writer lease or emit a non-lane-tagged event. Either trigger **refuses the lane and routes to the ephemeral-Camp clone**, so a *misclassified* "containable" experiment fails closed (denied the lane) instead of leaking; conformance asserts an experiment that attempts a baseline lease inside a lane is denied.
3. **Event-sourcing makes it cheap + safe.** Every injected fault + observed outcome is an event (`chaos.*`); a failed experiment yields a **replayable trace** that becomes a permanent regression test (the same pattern as AI live-fix, [Plan 23](../forge-generated-stack.md#plan-23)). Determinism ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)) means a discovered failure reproduces exactly.
4. **Continuous + gated.** Chaos runs in CI (against `runtime-local` + a Camp) and, optionally, **continuously in prod within a blast-radius + policy budget** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29) gate; error-budget-aware, [ADR-0045](#adr-0045)) — game-day-style, never unbounded.

### Alternatives considered
- **Keep ad-hoc fault-injection tests only.** Rejected: they assert specific cases but don't form a hypothesis-driven, continuous discipline that validates the resilience SLOs.
- **Adopt a chaos engine (Chaos Mesh/Litmus/FIS) as the model.** Rejected as the model (kept as optional providers): HoBo's faults are best injected at its *own* seams (actors/events/Executor/tokens) and recorded as events; external engines cover infra-level faults behind the `chaos` capability.
- **Prod chaos always-on by default.** Rejected: must be opt-in, blast-radius-bounded, and policy/error-budget-gated — chaos that ignores the budget is just an outage.

### Consequences
- **(+)** Closes #28: resilience claims ([ADR-0021](08-scope-and-requirements.md#adr-0021) RTO/RPO, auto-rollback, compensation) are *validated continuously*, not asserted; failures become replayable regression tests.
- **(+)** Reuses HoBo's failure taxonomy ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)), event-sourcing/replay ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)), policy gating ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)), and SLO/error-budget ([ADR-0045](#adr-0045)) — the `chaos` capability + `chaos.*` events are the only new surface; engines are optional providers.
- **(−)** Prod chaos is operationally sensitive; the blast-radius + budget gating must be airtight (a bad experiment is an incident). Mitigated by policy gating + abort conditions + air-gap-friendly local-first chaos.
- **(−)** Chaos coverage is only as good as the injectable seams; non-HoBo-mediated failures (e.g. a provider's internal bug) still need the external-engine providers.

## ADR-0060
**Compliance & data governance as a plane over the existing planes.**

### Context
HoBo composes its compliance raw materials (audit log, OCSF export, residency rules, retention windows, `hobo:crypto`, the policy plane) into one plane of **enforced** governance. Regulated buyers need: data placed where law requires, deleted on request, retained for the right window, and **provable** via attestations. Doing this per-app or per-cloud would be inconsistent and unauditable — so governance is a plane.

### Decision
1. **Classification is the keystone.** Resources and WIT-record fields carry classification labels ([ADR-0051](03-data-plane.md#adr-0051)); `data.Classified` feeds the policy plane's existing `dataClassifications`/`region` context ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)).
2. **Residency + retention are enforced as policy obligations** at **`can-deploy`** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) *and* runtime — reusing Matching region context ([ADR-0033](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0033)), cross-Camp residency ([ADR-0040](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0040)), and sovereign mode ([ADR-0036](07-portability-extensibility.md#adr-0036)) as the ceiling. Misplacement is a gate failure, not a runtime surprise.
3. **Right-to-erasure = crypto-shredding + projection purge, never history rewrite.** PII is encrypted per-subject via `hobo:crypto`; erasure destroys the key + writes a tombstone (`erasure.Requested/Completed`) and rebuilds projections excluding the subject ([Plan 32](../data-plane.md#plan-32)). The forward-only log invariant ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)) is preserved. **The guarantee is enforced, not assumed, and scoped to HoBo-controlled stores.** (a) **Coverage is gated:** `can-deploy`/conformance **rejects any WIT-record field labeled `pii`/`phi`/`pci` whose persisted/event payload is not provably routed through `hobo:crypto` under the subject key** ([ADR-0051](03-data-plane.md#adr-0051) classification + codegen), and a replay-time assertion proves **no plaintext PII for an erased subject is decodable post-shred** — a single mis-classified plaintext field is a gate failure, not a silent erasure hole. (b) **Scope is honest:** key destruction reaches every HoBo-controlled store/projection, but **data already exported past HoBo's boundary** (an external SIEM via OCSF, [ADR-0046](#adr-0046) §2; a hosted-model provider that saw a prompt) is **out of reach of the tombstone** — so PII-bearing exports are crypto-shred-keyed or excluded ([ADR-0046](#adr-0046) §2) and the erasure claim is "unreadable in HoBo-controlled stores," never "unreadable everywhere."
4. **Attestation bundles are assembled from the log** — SOC2/GDPR/HIPAA evidence is a signed projection over audit/OCSF + `policy.Decided` + `data.Classified`/`erasure.*`, produced by `hobo attest`; frameworks are declarative control→evidence packs.
5. **A subprocessor/DPA registry** is authoritative for who-processes-what-where; routing classified data to an unregistered/disallowed subprocessor fails `can-deploy`.

### Alternatives considered
- **Mutable/erasable event log (delete the user's events).** Rejected: destroys replay, audit, and time-travel — the core bet. Crypto-shredding gives deletion semantics without breaking the invariant.
- **Hand-maintained compliance spreadsheets / external GRC only.** Rejected as the source of truth: evidence drifts from reality. HoBo *generates* the evidence from facts and can still export to GRC tools.
- **Residency as advisory metadata.** Rejected: regulated data placement must *block* a non-compliant plan, not merely annotate it.
- **Per-app bespoke compliance.** Rejected: inconsistent and unauditable across a multi-cloud estate; governance belongs in a plane.

### Consequences
- **(+)** Turns scattered hints into enforced, provable governance with little new machinery — classification + obligations + crypto-shred + an evidence projection, all on existing planes.
- **(+)** Erasure that respects the immutable log is a genuinely hard problem solved coherently; attestations-from-the-log make audits cheaper and continuously fresh.
- **(+)** Strengthens the regulated-buyer / sovereign story already started by ADR-0036/0040.
- **(−)** Crypto-shredding requires disciplined per-subject key management and that PII actually be encrypted under those keys — now **enforced** by the §3 classification-coverage gate (a mis-classified plaintext PII field fails `can-deploy`) rather than left to discipline; a mis-scoped key boundary still widens erasure blast radius, and data exported past HoBo's boundary is explicitly out of erasure scope.
- **(−)** Attestation packs encode an interpretation of each framework's controls; they are evidence assembly, **not** a legal certification — that still requires an auditor. HoBo makes the evidence, not the opinion.

## ADR-0061
**AI safety & model governance: sandboxed execution, registry, guardrails, red-teaming.**

### Context
HoBo's differentiator is AI everywhere: trainable methods, the Forge, the Operator Crew, the generated stack. That makes the *governance* of models, prompts, and eval-datasets — and the *safety* of their outputs — load-bearing, and it makes the *execution environment* for AI work load-bearing too. Governance covers a registry for models/prompts, output moderation, adversarial red-teaming, and model cards / responsible-AI evidence, all enforced rather than advisory. Ungoverned, the differentiator is the biggest liability.

### Decision
1. **All AI work runs in sandboxed environments by default.** Every `agent()` / `"use training"` / Forge / generated-stack execution runs in an isolated, capability-scoped sandbox (the wasm runtime boundary, [Plan 20](../wasm-runtime-wit.md#plan-20)) with no ambient authority — network/filesystem/secret access is grant-only. Sandboxing is the enforced default, not an opt-in. **A hosted-model call is not an exception to grant-only egress.** A hosted-endpoint model is a first-class Registry entry ([Plan 6](../ai-native-agent-auth.md#plan-06)), but calling it needs network egress — so the entry **carries an explicit, residency-obligation-checked egress grant** ([ADR-0060](#adr-0060)): the egress to the provider is the **gated, audited boundary** (an un-granted hosted-model call is **denied by the sandbox**, and sending classified data to a non-residency-compliant endpoint fails the obligation), while the output guardrail (point 2) governs the *returned* output. Air-gap simply has no such grant, so it degrades to local models.
2. **Governance is enforced via industry toolkits as the default layer.** Moderation, guardrails, and safety run through **Microsoft's AI governance / Responsible AI toolkit** and **NVIDIA NeMo Guardrails** as the built-in, enforced default — wired into the `guardrail` capability ([Plan 21](../authoring-resources-wiring.md#plan-21)/[Plan 20](../wasm-runtime-wit.md#plan-20)) as a **policy-gated step** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) wrapping `agent()`/`"use training"`/generated outputs. These toolkits provide the default moderation/guardrail/safety layer (input/output filtering, topical rails, jailbreak defense, responsible-AI checks); they run local-first so air-gap keeps working. `guardrail.Blocked` + `policy.Decided` make every block replayable. **Streaming outputs are gated incrementally, not post-hoc** — the most common LLM delivery mode can't be allowed to leak tokens before moderation. The guardrail runs on a **buffered window with a commit boundary (a watermark): the model may not emit past the watermark until that window passes the rails**, so a disallowed span is blocked **before client delivery**, never retracted after it has been sent. Conformance asserts a disallowed token in a *streamed* response is withheld at the watermark, not emitted-then-corrected.
3. **The Registry pattern governs models/prompts/eval-datasets** ([Plan 6](../ai-native-agent-auth.md#plan-06)/[Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)): versioned, signed, provenance-bearing entries; prompts are governed because they are persisted code ([ADR-0048](06-ai-generation-operators.md#adr-0048)); promotion runs the four-lens gate.
4. **Red-teaming reuses the chaos tier** ([ADR-0047](#adr-0047)): hypothesis-driven, blast-radius-bounded, policy-gated adversarial experiments (`redteam.*`); a finding becomes a permanent regression test in a governed eval-dataset.
5. **Eval-datasets are governed artifacts** — classified ([Plan 46](../governance-planes.md#plan-46)), contamination-checked, extending the PII-not-trained rule ([ADR-0025](06-ai-generation-operators.md#adr-0025)) into a lifecycle.
6. **Model cards + responsible-AI attestations** are assembled from registry + guardrail + red-team + eval facts (the [Plan 46](../governance-planes.md#plan-46) attestation mechanism), signed and replayable.

### Alternatives considered
- **Run AI work with ambient authority (no sandbox).** Rejected: a generated/agent action with host access is an unbounded blast radius; sandboxed-by-default is the only safe grain for AI-native execution.
- **Guardrails behind an optional port with a thin built-in default.** Rejected: safety must be enforced, not optional. The Microsoft Responsible AI toolkit + NVIDIA NeMo Guardrails are the default governance layer, applied to every AI call as a policy-gated step.
- **Guardrails as library calls inside app code.** Rejected: not enforceable, not auditable, easy to bypass. A policy-gated step means "why blocked" is a `policy.Decided` and coverage is a promotion contract.
- **A bespoke model-management system separate from the Registry.** Rejected: duplicates signing/provenance/conformance; models are just another signed artifact class.
- **Trust the model provider's built-in safety only.** Rejected: not portable across providers, opaque, and useless air-gapped; the default governance layer runs local-first.
- **Red-teaming as a separate one-off tool.** Rejected: reusing the chaos tier gives it the same gated, event-sourced, finding→regression-test rigor for free.

### Consequences
- **(+)** The AI-native differentiator gets a matching trust story: sandboxed-by-default execution + an enforced governance layer (Microsoft Responsible AI, NeMo Guardrails) + registry entries + the chaos-tier pattern + attestation assembly.
- **(+)** Guardrail-coverage-as-contract means an "improved" prompt can't ship if it weakens safety; red-team findings harden the system permanently; the toolkits run local-first so air-gap keeps working.
- **(+)** Model governance composes with compliance ([Plan 46](../governance-planes.md#plan-46)) — dataset classification, weights residency, and responsible-AI attestations share one evidence pipeline.
- **(−)** The guardrail layer adds latency to every AI call, and streaming responses are gated at a **buffered-window watermark** (point 2) so a disallowed span is blocked before delivery — addressed by streaming-aware NeMo rails and a tunable window, at the cost of a small first-token latency for the buffered span.
- **(−)** Model cards encode an interpretation of evolving standards; like compliance bundles they are evidence, not legal certification.

## ADR-0039
**Metering, quota, cost attribution & cost-driven availability.**

### Context
The platform **attributes cloud cost**, enforces **quotas**, guards against **runaway spend**, and uses **cost as an availability lever** — independent of any commercial model. Money-movement (billing/invoicing/chargeback) is out of scope ([ADR-0064](08-scope-and-requirements.md#adr-0064)); this ADR is **FinOps / cost-safety**, not billing. [Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05) carries plan-time cost estimation, soft/hard **budgets**, **agent spend-caps**, and a **provider-resilience circuit-breaker**; this ADR adds the **metering** model (what's measurable + how), **quota** enforcement beyond budget alerts, a **runaway-spend** safeguard, a **cost-attribution / showback** pipeline, and the **cost↔availability link** that ties cost controls to the availability machinery ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05): leases/fencing, replication, circuit breakers, canary/auto-rollback, drift). For an AI-native control plane the right primitive is *"hold this availability target **within** this budget,"* not "pick one" — without the linkage, cost controls can themselves *cause* outages (a tripped breaker hangs requests) and the Planner would minimize cost unbounded by any availability floor.

### Decision
1. **Metering is an event-sourced projection.** A **`MeteringView`** folds **usage/cost counters** from the event log HoBo already keeps — `deployment.*`, `run.*`, `resource.*`, `forge.*`, `token.*`, and egress ([Plan 12](../security-networking.md#plan-12)/[Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)) — across a small dimension set: **control-plane operations, runtime-compute-seconds, storage-GB, egress-GB, and Forge-runs**. **The metered *quantities* ride existing events as carried fields — not a new pipeline.** Discrete facts already exist; the continuous measures are folded from **counters the terminal events carry** (e.g. `run.Completed.computeMs`, a byte counter on the egress/security-plane event, a periodic `resource.Metered{storageBytes}` heartbeat for stock quantities) — so "no new instrumentation" means **no new pipeline**, with the understanding that these quantity fields are part of the event payloads ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)). The view stays auditable, replayable, and rebuildable from zero like every projection, so **every cost figure traces to a carried fact**. The **Dispatcher**'s `CreditLedgerView` ([ADR-0071](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0071)) is the credit/budget-aware sibling of this view — it adds per-provider free-tier/promo/budget facts (`credit.*`) to derive an **effective marginal cost** for cost routing.
2. **Quotas + a runaway-spend circuit-breaker run through the policy plane.** A metered counter crossing a threshold produces a **`policy.Decided`** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)): **soft** → warn/alert obligation; **hard** → **deny at the gate** (block the spend-incurring action — deploy / agent provisioning / Forge run). **The hard quota cannot rely on the lagging projection alone.** `MeteringView` is an eventually-consistent fold (bounded-staleness, [ADR-0040](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0040)), so a hot-looping agent races ahead of the projection and the breaker would only trip *after* the spend is incurred. So a spend-incurring action is gated on a **synchronous pre-commit reservation**: the policy gate **decrements an at-issue quota counter (a fenced reservation) before the action runs**, and the `MeteringView` reconciles the actual cost afterward. The reservation bounds worst-case overshoot to **one in-flight action's reserved cost**, not "until the projection catches up." Excess **spend *velocity*** still trips a **durable spend circuit-breaker** — a saga obligation reusing the breaker pattern from [Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05) — that **halts spend-incurring actions until acknowledged/reset**. The existing **agent spend-caps** ([Plan 6](../ai-native-agent-auth.md#plan-06)/[Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)) generalize to **tenant / Camp** quotas.
3. **Showback (cost attribution, not money).** Cost is attributed by the dimensions already in the event envelope — **(platform) tenant → project → Camp → Bindle → run/agent/team**, and (for a multi-tenant app) **also by `app-tenant`** ([Plan 50](../multi-tenant-apps.md#plan-50)) — and surfaced as **showback** (visibility). The cost/showback/allocation abstraction is **modeled on the [OpenCost](https://opencost.io) open standard** (the CNCF cost-allocation spec) so figures are portable and interoperate with existing FinOps tooling rather than being a bespoke shape. Per-app-tenant showback gives the developer the cost/usage input to run *their own* billing — but HoBo itself ships **no chargeback/invoicing**, and **does not bill the app's tenants** ([ADR-0064](08-scope-and-requirements.md#adr-0064)/[ADR-0079](02-identity-security.md#adr-0079)): showback + quotas are in, charging money is the developer's business. It is OSS-core and self-hostable like the rest of the platform.
4. **Cost is an availability lever: graceful degradation before hard-stop.** Cost controls **shed quality-of-service gracefully** rather than hard-stopping, and provisioning optimizes cost **within** an availability floor. A declarative **`costAvailabilityProfile`** per Camp/Bindle binds a budget to an availability target with ordered **degradation tiers**:
   ```ts
   costAvailability({
     budgetUsd: 5000,                 // period hard cap
     availabilitySlo: 0.999,          // nominal target ([ADR-0045](#adr-0045))
     tiers: [                         // entered as spend velocity approaches the cap
       { name: "nominal", upToBudgetFrac: 0.80, slo: 0.999 },
       { name: "reduced", upToBudgetFrac: 0.95, slo: 0.99,  shed: ["batch", "analytics"] },
       { name: "minimal", upToBudgetFrac: 1.00, slo: 0.95,  shed: ["non-critical"] },
     ],
     strategy: "shed-low-priority-first",   // | "degrade-all" | "fail-closed"
   })
   ```
   Resources/handlers carry a **`priority`/criticality** label (`critical`|`standard`|`low`); tiers shed lowest-priority load first. The `MeteringView` feeds **real-time spend velocity** into the policy plane, so a `policy.Decided` can ask *"at this velocity, do we cross tier N before period end?"* and yield `allow` (fits nominal) · **`allow-degraded`** + obligation (*"enter reduced tier; shed batch"*) · `deny`/`fail-closed` (would breach the hard cap). Crossing a tier is a **durable saga obligation, not a halt** (`spend.DegradationRequired`): the **SRE operator** ([Plan 40](../operators-sre.md#plan-40)) + runtime enforce it by composing existing levers — **scale-to-zero** non-critical workloads ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05) wasm economics), **feature-flag shed** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)), **cache-TTL increase / expensive-query shed**, **request-priority shedding** (an honest `503` + `Retry-After` to low-priority callers, never a silent hang), and **canary hold**. The **runaway-spend circuit-breaker (point 2) is the last resort** *after* graceful tiers are exhausted — degradation is the path *to* it, not a synonym for it; recovery emits `spend.DegradationRolledBack` when velocity falls. The Planner objective ([ADR-0033](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0033)) becomes *"minimize cost **subject to** the availability floor"* — the cheapest realization (provider/topology) that still meets `availabilitySlo`, with the cost **and** SLO rationale recorded in `match.Decided`/`deployment.Planned`. A cost-induced degradation is a first-class incident class ([Plan 40](../operators-sre.md#plan-40)): the SRE operator may *propose* cost-efficient remediation (*"hold 99% to stay under budget while the leak is fixed"*) as a gated obligation, and postmortem-by-replay shows exactly which load was shed and when.
5. **Events:** `spend.CircuitTripped` · `spend.CircuitReset` · `spend.DegradationRequired` · `spend.DegradationApplied` · `spend.DegradationRolledBack`. **Quota + degradation decisions ride the existing `policy.Decided`** + saga/obligation model (no new engine); metering itself is a projection (no per-usage events).

### Alternatives considered
- **A separate metering pipeline (independent instrumentation).** Rejected: duplicates what the log records and risks drift between "what happened" and "what was metered"; the projection is the source-of-truth-consistent answer.
- **A bespoke quota subsystem** separate from the policy plane. Rejected: duplicates the decision model + gates and splits "why was this blocked" across two systems.
- **Soft alerts only (no hard enforcement).** Rejected: no protection against runaway spend (a looping agent racking up cost).
- **Keep cost and availability independent.** Rejected: the breaker can cause the outage it's meant to prevent, and the Planner has no availability floor — the exact "availability through cost controls" gap.
- **Hard-stop on budget only (no degradation tiers).** Rejected: turns a cost event into a binary outage; tiers + priority shedding preserve critical traffic.
- **A new cost-degradation engine.** Rejected: reuse the policy plane (obligations), feature flags, the SRE operator, scale-to-zero, and the spend circuit-breaker — the cost↔availability link is a composition + schema, not new runtime.

### Consequences
- **(+)** The platform can meter, quota, protect against runaway spend, attribute cost, and **hold an availability target within a budget** — all from the existing log + policy plane, with auditable figures; agents/operators can target *"99.9% within $5k,"* and cost pressure degrades QoS gracefully instead of failing.
- **(+)** Reuses the event log/projection model ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)), the policy plane + obligations ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)), the circuit-breaker pattern ([Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)), feature flags, the SRE operator, the Planner objective, and agent spend-caps ([Plan 6](../ai-native-agent-auth.md#plan-06)) — little new machinery (a projection + a profile + a Planner constraint + the `spend.*` events).
- **(+)** Honest failure modes (priority shedding + `503`+`Retry-After`) instead of silent hangs under budget pressure.
- **(−)** Metering fidelity depends on the cost-dimension model; mapping raw events → fair, intuitive counters is ongoing work.
- **(−)** A spend circuit-breaker that halts actions is a sharp safeguard, and tier thresholds + velocity windows need tuning to avoid false trips and flapping between tiers while still catching real runaways.
- **(−)** Degradation tiers + priority labels are new authoring surface; sensible defaults (one nominal tier, fail-closed) keep it optional.

## ADR-0076
**Boundary virtualization: stubs/links carry a mode for request-level isolation, preview, self-healing & non-destructive chaos.**

- **Origin:** the suite had large gaps in *how devs test* and how the DX reduces test friction. Since every boundary already carries a **PACT contract** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) and is a **link** at the wRPC membrane ([Plan 20](../wasm-runtime-wit.md#plan-20)/[ADR-0069](01-architecture-core.md#adr-0069)), one mechanism can auto-mock a system-under-test's out-of-scope boundaries, do **bridge-to-kubernetes-style request-level isolation** (forked services per branch/dev in a shared env), let AI self-healing safely experiment, and run chaos without taking real services down. Designed via brainstorming; decisions: **no new noun — extend stubs/links/environments/chaos**; **request-level isolation is the default** (full ephemeral-Camp clone stays available).

### Context
HoBo already has the pieces — **stubs** (typed recording placeholders behind a WIT port, [Plan 23](../forge-generated-stack.md#plan-23)), **PACT contracts** on every boundary in four modes ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)), **links** that late-bind import→export with the membrane routing *only linked* interfaces ([Plan 20](../wasm-runtime-wit.md#plan-20)), **shadow traffic + champion/challenger + gated canary/auto-rollback** ([Plan 23](../forge-generated-stack.md#plan-23)/[Plan 5](../reliability-observability-cost.md#plan-05)), the **`chaos` capability** ([ADR-0047](#adr-0047)), and **ephemeral per-PR Camps** ([Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)). What was missing: a single statement that a *boundary* can be virtualized — served by something other than the live provider — and that this same capability is the substrate for testing, preview environments, self-healing experiments, and non-destructive chaos. The friction today: testing a service means standing up the whole system or hand-writing mocks; there is no first-class "fork just my service into the real shared environment" path.

### Decision
1. **A link carries a boundary *mode* (the stub mechanism, generalized).** For any boundary (a link, [Plan 20](../wasm-runtime-wit.md#plan-20)), the host can serve it as: **`live`** (the real provider, default) · **`mock`** (a contract-conformant stub auto-served from the boundary's PACT contract + recorded traces — no hand-written mocks) · **`record`/`replay`** (capture real traffic, replay deterministically) · **`fork`** (route to an overriding impl) · **`shadow`** (duplicate traffic to a candidate, discard its response) · **`fault`** (inject latency/error/drop via the `chaos` capability). This is **not a new subsystem** — `mock`/`record`/`replay` *are* the existing stub ([Plan 23](../forge-generated-stack.md#plan-23)); `shadow` *is* the existing eval/champion-challenger path ([Plan 23](../forge-generated-stack.md#plan-23)); `fault` *is* `chaos` ([ADR-0047](#adr-0047)) scoped to a boundary; the decision unifies them as modes on the link seam.
2. **Request-level isolation is the default (a sandbox lane), with an ephemeral-Camp clone available.** A **lane** = a *selector* + a set of boundary-mode overrides, overlaid on a **shared baseline environment**; matching requests route to the lane's forks/mocks, all other traffic stays baseline — the **bridge-to-kubernetes / request-level-isolation** model (*one shared env, no full clone*). **Lane membership is a signed lane token bound to the requesting principal + tenant** (SVID/`ctx.user`, [Plan 6](../ai-native-agent-auth.md#plan-06)/[Plan 12](../security-networking.md#plan-12)), **not a bare header** — so it can't be spoofed to reach another dev's fork or to escape isolation. It is **enforced fail-closed at every wRPC membrane hop** *and* **carried on the `EventEnvelope` (`lane`) for async** (the *Robustness* section below): a sync hop or async subscriber that can't prove in-lane provenance **denies — it never falls through to the baseline**. The heavier **ephemeral-Camp clone** ([Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)) remains for full isolation.
3. **PACT makes mock/fork safe — checked at lane join, not only at deploy.** A `mock` *serves* the boundary contract (the SUT exercises the real expected shape). A `fork` must **satisfy the boundary's consumer-driven contract before any baseline traffic routes to it** — a **lightweight runtime contract-check at lane join** (the fork's exported contract verified against the baseline consumers' expectations), *distinct from* the deploy-time `can-deploy` ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) since a `hobo dev --fork` never goes through Delta→Set. A fork that fails the check is refused the lane. Contracts bound *shape*, **not behavior or resource use** — fidelity (R9) and blast radius (R5) are handled separately.
4. **Writes are lane-scoped — and baseline aggregates are lane-aware, not just the fork.** A lane's writes go to **lane-scoped overlays** (isolated streams + projection sinks, [Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)/[Plan 32](../data-plane.md#plan-32)). Because state is a **single-writer aggregate folding its own stream** ([Plan 32](../data-plane.md#plan-32)), isolation can't be left to "forks write overlays" — a *baseline* service serving a lane request would otherwise read/append the *real* stream. So **any aggregate a lane request touches forks-on-touch**: it folds the baseline stream up to the fork point, then appends the lane's events to a **lane tail** (read-your-writes within the lane; the baseline stream is never appended under a lane), and a lane request may **never take a baseline single-writer lease**. **Side-effecting external boundaries** (`notify`/payments/real provider writes, [Plan 21](../authoring-resources-wiring.md#plan-21)) have no overlay, so they **default to `mock`/`record` inside a lane** (deny-by-default for real external side effects unless the lane is explicitly granted `live` egress). Overlays are reaped with the lane and are in the retention/erasure scope ([ADR-0060](#adr-0060)).
5. **The same mechanism serves four consumers + preview:** **(a) test isolation** — `hobo test`/`hobo sandbox` runs a SUT with out-of-scope boundaries auto-`mock`ed from contracts, or `fork`s just the SUT into a shared baseline ([Plan 9](../verification.md#plan-09)); **(b) preview environments** — first-class per-PR: a lane over a shared baseline (forked changed services + mocked/real rest) *or* a cheap ephemeral Camp, with a preview URL, lane-scoped writes, reaped on merge/TTL ([Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)); **(c) AI self-healing experiments** — the SRE/SWE operator tries a candidate fix on a `shadow`/`fork` lane against shadowed real traffic, scores it (evals), and promotes only via the gated canary ([Plan 23](../forge-generated-stack.md#plan-23)/[Plan 40](../operators-sre.md#plan-40)) — the safe-experiment substrate that advances open **Q#13** ([Plan 9](../verification.md#plan-09)); **(d) non-destructive chaos** — `fault` injected into a lane bounds the blast radius to that lane, so the steady-state hypothesis is tested without faulting the real service ([ADR-0047](#adr-0047)).
6. **Mostly existing families — corrected routing, minimal new facts.** Lane lifecycle is an `env.*` overlay (`env.Cloned`/`env.Reaped`); a boundary mode rides `link.Established`; `mock`/`record` reuse `stub.Recorded`/`link.Called`; `shadow` reuses `impl.Shadowed`/`eval.*`; `fault` reuses `chaos.*` ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)). **Routing is *not* a per-request `policy.Decided`** (that is a deploy/gate-time decision model, [Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)) — it is the **signed lane token enforced at the membrane** + `EventEnvelope.lane` (point 2 / R1). **Lane-origin events are tagged with the lane id and excluded by default** from observed-contract promotion ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)), training trajectories ([Plan 23](../forge-generated-stack.md#plan-23)), and tenant metering rollups ([ADR-0039](#adr-0039)) — a fork's experimental/faulty behavior never poisons a baseline contract, a training reward, or a bill.

### Robustness hardening
The mechanism above is clean on the *synchronous* link seam; an adversarial review surfaced that HoBo's *asynchronous, event-sourced* seam (the log, `topic`/`queue`, the Reactor, sagas) and several isolation guarantees needed explicit hardening, since the async seam dominates this architecture. The load-bearing fixes:
- **R1 — Async isolation is first-class, on the *replay* path as well as live dispatch.** The lane selector is a field of the **`EventEnvelope` (`lane`)** ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)); **every subscriber/Reactor/saga/queue dispatch filters by lane, deny-by-default** — a baseline subscriber **ignores** lane events (so a forked `orders` service's emitted `Order.Placed` can't make baseline `billing` charge a real card), while a lane subscriber sees lane events + baseline read-through. **Crucially this is an invariant of the *fold/replay* path too, not only live dispatch:** because authoritativeness is "rebuild-from-zero == live state" ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)), a **baseline projection/aggregate rebuild excludes `lane`-tagged events by default** — otherwise an upcaster or an older lane-unaware projection that re-folds the whole log would silently fold a fork's experimental/faulty events into baseline state. Conformance asserts a **from-zero baseline rebuild over a log containing lane events equals the lane-free rebuild**. Without this (on both paths) the "can't corrupt shared state" guarantee is false in an event-sourced system — this is the single biggest fix.
- **R2 — Fail-closed, signed, principal/tenant-bound membership — and short-lived/revocable, not just unspoofable** (point 2): no silent baseline fallthrough on a dropped/absent selector; the token is verified at each membrane hop and can't be spoofed; a lane is **strictly tenant-scoped** (the token carries + enforces the tenant claim, read-through is filtered by the *original request's* tenant — no confused-deputy cross-tenant read, [Plan 12](../security-networking.md#plan-12)/[ADR-0023](02-identity-security.md#adr-0023)). **"Can't be spoofed" is not "can't be replayed":** the lane token is **bound to the lane TTL with short-lived rotating credentials + a nonce/expiry**, and the membrane checks a **`lane.Revoked` revocation signal** — so a leaked token grants at most a bounded, revocable window onto that lane, not unbounded access. Conformance asserts an **expired or revoked lane token denies fail-closed** (never falls through to baseline).
- **R3 — Lane-aware aggregates + external-side-effect default-mock, with `live` egress itself gated** (point 4): fork-on-touch, write-goes-to-overlay, no baseline lease, real egress denied in-lane by default. The default-mock is necessary but not sufficient — a lane *can* be granted `live` external egress, so that grant is **not a free flag**: any `live` external-egress grant inside a lane **must itself pass a deny-by-default policy gate** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)), be **audited** (`policy.Decided`), and run under the **R5 blast-radius bound**. Assertion: **no `notify`/payment/real-write side effect fires from a lane lacking an explicit, gated `live` grant** — so a misclassified preview/self-heal lane can't quietly send a real notification or charge a card.
- **R4 — Read-through honors data governance** ([ADR-0060](#adr-0060)): a preview/fork reading baseline data passes the same classification/residency obligation as any runtime read; **`restricted` classes default to masked/synthetic** in a lane; lane overlays are in the crypto-shred/retention model.
- **R5 — Resource blast-radius is bounded** (contracts bound shape, not resource use): a lane runs under a **resource budget** (rate/concurrency/lease-acquisition/queue-depth caps) + isolation tier ([ADR-0012](02-identity-security.md#adr-0012)) + an abort/circuit-breaker, so a hot-looping or poison-message fork can't degrade the shared baseline — the same blast-radius discipline [ADR-0047](#adr-0047) requires of `fault`, now also of `fork`.
- **R6 — Deterministic replay requires recording read-through.** A lane mixes recorded mocks with **live** baseline reads, which are non-deterministic; so a lane's baseline read-through is **captured at the boundary (value + observed `seq`)** like a `record` mode — a lane run replays against that capture, or it is **not** claimed replayable (corrects the earlier over-claim).
- **R7 — Self-heal/chaos lanes take the strictest isolation** ([ADR-0047](#adr-0047)/[Plan 23](../forge-generated-stack.md#plan-23)): no async egress to baseline subscribers, no shared-lease acquisition; if a candidate fix or fault can't be contained to the lane, it routes to an **ephemeral-Camp clone** instead of a shared-baseline lane — so an experiment/fault never leaks into prod via a shared subscriber or lease.
- **R8 — Lifecycle: TTL + drain-before-reap.** A lane has a TTL; reap **quiesces in-flight requests + fails/migrates running sagas** before discarding the overlay (no saga left mid-compensation with a vanished overlay); overlays have a size cap with backpressure ([Plan 32](../data-plane.md#plan-32)); a lane is a **metering dimension** ([ADR-0039](#adr-0039)).
- **R9 — Mock fidelity is labeled; `hobo verify` stays honest** ([ADR-0042](07-portability-extensibility.md#adr-0042)): a `mock` is contract-conformant (*shape*), not behaviorally complete, so an auto-mock result carries the ADR-0042 fidelity label (`mock`/`simulate`) and **`hobo verify` refuses a prod-ready claim from an all-mock run** — exactly as for unrun `camp` checks; `mock` fidelity is graded by contract mode (declared < observed/promoted < replay-of-recorded). Honest message: *"passed against mocks, not the real service."*
- **R10 — Propagation is a runtime control, not a test.** "Conformance asserts baggage propagation" is reserved for verifying each **port adapter forwards the lane field**; the *system* guarantee is the fail-closed signed token (R2) enforced at the membrane — a runtime mechanism, not a unit test.

### Alternatives considered
- **A new standalone test/sandbox plane (a named noun).** Rejected (per the design choice): it is a *capability of the existing stub/link/contract/env machinery*, not a new subsystem — naming it a plane would imply a parallel mechanism it doesn't need.
- **Environment-clone-per-branch as the default** (the Vercel-preview model). Rejected as the default: heavier, slower, and lower-fidelity to the real shared system than request-level isolation; kept as the available heavier option ([Plan 25](../platform-engineering.md#plan-25)).
- **Hand-written mocks / a mocking library.** Rejected: the PACT contract + recorded traces already define the boundary, so mocks are *generated*, not authored — and stay in sync with the contract by construction.
- **Let forks share baseline state.** Rejected: a branch experiment could corrupt shared data; lane-scoped copy-on-write overlays + read-through keep isolation while still seeing real upstream state.
- **A separate chaos-only injector and a separate mock-only tool.** Rejected: chaos (`fault`), mocking (`mock`), and forking (`fork`) are the *same* "serve this boundary differently" operation at the *same* link seam; unifying them is the point.

### Consequences
- **(+)** Testing friction drops sharply: a dev `fork`s one service into the **real** shared environment isolated by their branch/identity, or runs it with every dependency auto-`mock`ed from contracts — no full env stand-up, no hand-written mocks.
- **(+)** One mechanism, four payoffs: the *same* boundary-mode capability gives test sandboxes, cheap preview environments, safe self-healing experiments, and non-destructive chaos — each reusing stubs/contracts/links/chaos/envs rather than a new system.
- **(+)** Safe by construction **across both seams** (with R1–R10): sync *and* async boundaries are lane-isolated (deny-by-default, **fail-closed**, signed + tenant-bound), baseline aggregates **fork-on-touch**, real external side effects are denied in-lane, resource blast radius is bounded, and read-through is governed + recorded. The guarantee is explicit + mechanism-backed, not assumed — and the earlier sync-only "safe by construction" claim is now true on the event-sourced seam too.
- **(+)** Advances open **Q#13** — AI self-healing now has a **safe place to experiment** (a strictly-isolated shadow/fork lane, R7) before any prod canary; the "low-risk auto" threshold governs *promotion from lane → canary*, not unguarded prod edits.
- **(−)** Async lane-filtering touches **every** subscriber/Reactor/saga/queue dispatch + the `EventEnvelope` — real surface to build and conformance-cover; a dispatcher that ignores `lane` is a leak, so the filter is a **runtime invariant, not opt-in**.
- **(−)** Fork-on-touch of baseline aggregates + recorded read-through add cost/complexity to the data plane; deep stateful branch tests may still warrant the ephemeral-Camp clone (the documented heavier path).
- **(−)** The friction win is real, but the *safe* version is heavier than the first sketch: a lane is a signed, tenant-scoped, resource-budgeted, governed overlay — more machinery than "route a header," which is the price of doing it correctly on an event-sourced, multi-tenant substrate.

## ADR-0077
**Projects & environments as grouping dimensions; the operations dashboard & per-project status pages.**

- **Origin:** the ask — *a default dashboard like Aspire but for multi-cloud / multi-environment deployments, and a default status page for every app/project/collection-of-deployables showing aggregate downtime + dependent services.* Designed via brainstorming. Full surface in [Plan 49](../dashboards-status-availability.md#plan-49).

### Context
HoBo's scope chain `Bindle ⊂ Camp ⊂ Yard ⊂ Junction` ([ADR-0026](01-architecture-core.md#adr-0026)) is **infra ownership**, but a real product is "many services across many clouds and many environments" — a grouping that **cross-cuts** that chain. The developer dashboard ([Plan 7](../developer-experience.md#plan-07)) is authoring-focused; there is no default **operations** surface that rolls up health/SLO/incidents/cost across clouds and environments, and no **status** surface (aggregate uptime + dependent-service health) for stakeholders. All the raw material exists — SLO/error-budget ([ADR-0045](#adr-0045)), incidents ([ADR-0058](06-ai-generation-operators.md#adr-0058)), cost/showback ([ADR-0039](#adr-0039)), the dependency/link graph ([Plan 21](../authoring-resources-wiring.md#plan-21)/[Plan 20](../wasm-runtime-wit.md#plan-20)), the env-group ([ADR-0028](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0028)) — but it is not composed into operator- or stakeholder-facing surfaces, and the grouping concepts a multi-Bindle product needs are unnamed.

### Decision
1. **Project = a grouping dimension over Bindles, not a fifth infra scope.** A **project** names an app/product/portfolio that spans many Bindles, Camps, Yards, and Junctions. It is **recorded on events like `tenant`** ([Plan 12](../security-networking.md#plan-12)) — an ownership / observability / RBAC rollup axis — **never inserted into `Bindle ⊂ Camp ⊂ Yard ⊂ Junction`** (which stays pure infra containment, [ADR-0026](01-architecture-core.md#adr-0026)). A Bindle declares its project; the dimension is inherited by every event its resources/runs emit. A project is an RBAC/ownership boundary in the `hobo:*` taxonomy ([ADR-0074](02-identity-security.md#adr-0074)) — **but, being also an authz boundary, it is constrained**: a project **lives inside one `tenant` and never spans tenants**, project names are **namespaced per tenant**, RBAC over a project is evaluated **within** the owning tenant, **re-assigning** a Bindle's project is a **privileged, audited `policy.Decided`** (it moves resources across an RBAC boundary), and the **singleton-project default inherits the Bindle owner's grants** (a missing/mutated tag can't widen access). **Every rollup filters `tenant` first**, then `project`/`environment`; an `environment`-axis rollup is intersected with the caller's grants so same-environment-type Camps of another project/tenant are never co-mingled.
2. **Environment = the env-group elevated to a rollup axis.** A named environment (`production`/`staging`) is the set of Camps of that **environment type** ([ADR-0028](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0028)), possibly across providers/Yards. It already exists as the multi-cloud deploy unit; here it becomes a **filter/rollup axis**. The operational grid is **project × environment**, drillable Project → Bindle → Camp → resource.
3. **A default operations dashboard — projection-only, runtime-agnostic, off the request path.** The fleet view (the Aspire analog for prod, [`competitors/aspire`](../../../competitors/aspire.md)) composes fleet topology (the link map + hop class), health/SLO, incidents, deployments, cost, and architecture availability ([ADR-0078](#adr-0078)), each a **read-only projection** over the log ([Plan 11](../event-model.md#plan-11)) scoped by project × environment. It reuses the developer dashboard's projection model ([Plan 7](../developer-experience.md#plan-07)), never sits on the data-plane request path ([Plan 4](../control-plane.md#plan-04)), and is RBAC-governed per project ([ADR-0074](02-identity-security.md#adr-0074)).
4. **A default status page per project — internal by default, opt-in public, redacted.** Every project gets a status page (aggregate uptime/downtime, **dependent-service health**, incident history + postmortem-as-replay [ADR-0058](06-ai-generation-operators.md#adr-0058); the [ADR-0078](#adr-0078) architecture estimate is **internal-only**), drillable to Bindle, filterable by environment. **Internal by default;** publishing public is a deliberate, curated act with a **hard, conformance-gated redaction contract**: only **declared public components** (an allowlist mapping public component → internal service set) are shown; internal service/provider/Camp/cost identifiers **and the entire [ADR-0078](#adr-0078) estimate** (weakest-link/hop-class/per-provider 9s/hints) are redacted; the public page shows only a **coarse rolled-up *observed* availability per public component** + curated incidents (`statuspage.IncidentPosted` free text passes the redaction scan before publish). It **propagates the bounded-staleness label** ([ADR-0040](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0040)) and renders **"delayed/unknown" rather than a confident stale "operational"** under partition. Served on a custom domain ([ADR-0035](07-portability-extensibility.md#adr-0035)) with no auth. The **conformance gate** is a denylist scan over the *rendered* output (incl. incident free text) + allowlist fuzz; each served snapshot's **content hash** + a `statuspage.Published` **re-emitted on every allowlist/profile change** make "what was shown publicly, when" provable, not merely "what was declared publishable."
5. **Minimal events; reuse `notify` for delivery.** Grouping adds **no** event family (`project` is a recorded dimension; `environment` derives from Camp type). Status pages add only `statuspage.Published`/`Unpublished`/`IncidentPosted`; subscriber notification rides the `notify` port ([ADR-0059](03-data-plane.md#adr-0059)), not a re-built mechanism.

### Alternatives considered
- **Make `project` a fifth scope noun (`Project ⊃ Junction`…).** Rejected: it would corrupt the infra-containment chain ([ADR-0026](01-architecture-core.md#adr-0026)) — a project spans *peer* Junctions/Yards and owns none of them. A cross-cutting recorded dimension (like `tenant`) is the honest model.
- **Extend the developer dashboard ([Plan 7](../developer-experience.md#plan-07)) instead of a distinct operations surface.** Rejected: the authoring loop and the cross-cloud fleet/stakeholder views have different consumers, scoping, and redaction needs; they share the *projection model*, not the surface.
- **Public status page by default.** Rejected: exposing internal topology/health by default is a security and privacy footgun; public is an explicit, redacted publish.
- **Build a status-page notification/subscription product.** Rejected as scope ([ADR-0064](08-scope-and-requirements.md#adr-0064)): emit `statuspage.IncidentPosted`; the `notify` port delivers.

### Consequences
- **(+)** A product gets a default cross-cloud, cross-environment operations view and a per-project status page with **no setup** — projections over data HoBo already has.
- **(+)** `project`/`environment` give a clean rollup for observability, cost, *and* governance without a new infra scope; they compose with metering ([ADR-0039](#adr-0039)) and RBAC ([ADR-0074](02-identity-security.md#adr-0074)).
- **(+)** The public page can't leak internal topology by construction (allowlist + redaction contract + audited disclosure facts).
- **(−)** `project` is a new tagging dimension every event carries and every Bindle declares — small but suite-wide; an un-tagged Bindle defaults to a singleton project and a `hobo doctor` nudge.
- **(−)** The redaction contract is a maintained surface (a new public-component mapping must be reviewed); a mis-mapped component could over-expose, so publishing is gated + audited.

## ADR-0078
**Architecture-derived availability — the "march of the 9s."**

- **Origin:** the ask — *expose estimated downtime due to architecture; calculate the "march of the 9s" through network hops for each service.* Designed via brainstorming. Full surface in [Plan 49](../dashboards-status-availability.md#plan-49) §D.

### Context
HoBo knows each app's **dependency graph** (the link map + bind edges, [Plan 21](../authoring-resources-wiring.md#plan-21)/[Plan 20](../wasm-runtime-wit.md#plan-20)), each resource's **provider** and **replication topology** ([ADR-0040](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0040)), and each provider's **declared capabilities** ([ADR-0009](02-identity-security.md#adr-0009)/[ADR-0075](07-portability-extensibility.md#adr-0075)). That is exactly the information needed to *estimate* availability from architecture — yet the platform only reports **observed** uptime after the fact. An estimate makes the reliability cost of an architecture (long serial chains, cross-cloud hops, missing redundancy) visible **before** an outage and quantifies "what a replica buys."

### Decision
1. **An `AvailabilityView` projection composes availability over the dependency graph.** Pure function of `(dependency-graph snapshot, declared SLAs, topology, observed window)` — replayable/explainable like every HoBo decision. No new event types.
2. **Two inputs: published SLAs (design-time) + observed (calibration).** Each provider/resource declares an **availability SLA** in its Capability Manifest/Spec ([ADR-0009](02-identity-security.md#adr-0009)/[ADR-0075](07-portability-extensibility.md#adr-0075)); an unknown SLA is a **declared "unrated,"** never silently 100%. Observed availability (SLO/health + `incident.*`, [ADR-0045](#adr-0045)/[ADR-0058](06-ai-generation-operators.md#adr-0058)) is shown **alongside** the estimate; the **estimate-vs-observed gap is a first-class signal**.
3. **Composition rules — a defined algorithm over a real DAG, not a naïve path product.** A **serial dependency** multiplies availabilities (`∏aᵢ` → **fewer 9s**); a **redundant set** composes parallel (`1 − ∏(1 − aᵢ)` → **more 9s**), but **active-active uses the parallel formula directly while active-passive/failover applies a coverage/failover-success factor** (detection + switch time + failover-can-fail), so a passive replica isn't over-credited ([ADR-0040](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0040)); **each network hop is a failure domain** classed **in-Camp / cross-Camp / cross-cloud** whose availability is a **declared input** (hop-class default in the Capability Manifest, overridable per link, [ADR-0009](02-identity-security.md#adr-0009); undeclared ⇒ "unrated"), so a cross-cloud hop visibly costs 9s. **Well-definedness:** the graph (shared sub-dependencies, diamonds, components on both serial and redundant paths) is **reduced to series-parallel form, else a minimal-cut-set computation**; a **shared sub-dependency is composed once (memoized per component, not per path)**; **cycles are rejected and flagged** (a dependency cycle is itself a finding). Output = estimated availability + **estimated annual downtime** per service, rolled up per Bindle/environment/project, with a **weakest-link path** and **"+N 9s if you add a replica here"** hints.
4. **The estimate is an upper bound, comparative guidance — not an SLA; gating is warn-only on the *observed* number.** It is a pure projection (replayable), but §D's limits mean it is an *upper bound on availability* used for relative comparison + redundancy hints, never an absolute prediction. So its tie to the availability floor is **warn-only**: an architecture whose *estimate* can't clear the **nominal `availabilitySlo`** (the top tier of the `costAvailabilityProfile` — distinct from that profile's *runtime* degradation ladder, [ADR-0039](#adr-0039)) raises a **`can-deploy` warning** ([Plan 29](../contracts-policy-flags.md#plan-29)); the **hard deploy gate keys on the *observed* SLO, not the estimate** — design-time reliability feedback, not a block on an optimistic number.

### Alternatives considered
- **Observed uptime only (no design-time estimate).** Rejected: answers "what was our downtime" but not "what does our architecture imply" — and gives no pre-outage signal or "what does a replica buy" answer, which is the ask.
- **Pure SLA estimate, no observed calibration.** Rejected: SLA composition ignores correlated failures, grey failures, and human/deploy outages; without observed calibration the number would be quietly wrong. Showing both + the gap is honest.
- **Full fault-tree / common-cause modeling now.** Deferred (iteration hook): serial/parallel composition over the real graph is the high-value first cut; correlated-failure modeling is a refinement, not v1.
- **Assume 100% for unrated components.** Rejected: silently inflates the estimate; "unrated" is surfaced.

### Consequences
- **(+)** "Estimated downtime due to architecture" becomes a real, explainable number per service/app/environment/project — the march of the 9s through the actual hop graph, rewarding declared redundancy ([ADR-0040](05-config-matching-environments.md#adr-0040)) and pricing cross-cloud hops.
- **(+)** Reuses the dependency graph, declared SLAs, topology, and observed SLO/incident data — a projection + a composition algorithm, no new runtime or events.
- **(+)** Estimate-vs-observed gap is a diagnostic: architecture under-delivering ⇒ a reliability bug to find; over-delivering ⇒ conservative SLAs.
- **(−)** The estimate is only as good as declared SLAs + the modeled serial/parallel structure; correlated/grey/human failures aren't captured by SLA composition — mitigated by always showing observed alongside and surfacing the gap (never presenting the estimate as truth).
- **(−)** Declared availability SLAs become another conformance-covered field providers must supply (or be marked "unrated"), and keeping them current is ongoing.
- **(−)** Real dependency graphs aren't series-parallel, so the composition needs a reduction step (series-parallel else minimal-cut-set) + memoized shared sub-dependencies + cycle rejection — a defined, conformance-covered algorithm, not a one-line product.
