# Bo's sprite sheets

Supplied artwork, same rule as the scenery plates: dropped in here and used as
given. Nothing in this folder is generated by the site.

| File | What it is | Grid | Present |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bo-walk.png` | walking east (screen-right) | 6×6, 36 frames | yes |
| `bo-walk-left.png` | walking west (screen-left) | 6×6, 36 frames | yes |
| `bo-idle.png` | rest, bounce, blink | 12×1 strip | yes |
| `bo-stand.png` | rest, breath, blink — a neutral stand | 12×1 strip | yes |

`bo-idle.png` and `bo-stand.png` share one strip contract — 12 frames of
207×230, frame 0 rest, 1–10 the cycle, 11 blink — so `<BoIdle variant="stand" />`
swaps the image and changes nothing else. The difference is register: idle's
cycle is a hop, stand's is a breath anchored at the feet, for places where Bo
should be present without performing.

`<ParallaxTrail facing="west" />` fails the build if its sheet is missing. That
is deliberate: there is no fallback, because the obvious fallback is wrong — see
below.

## The west sheet cannot be a mirror

Bo is not symmetric. **The bindle hangs from one specific anatomical arm**, and
horizontally flipping the east sheet hands it to the other one. It looks
plausible at a glance and it is wrong, which is the worst combination; it
shipped here once before this note existed.

Two persistent limb identities, which never swap between directions:

- **Bindle arm** — the arm that holds the pole in `bo-walk.png`. It holds the
  pole in every frame of every sheet. It is never the other arm.
- **Free arm** — the other one. It never holds or touches the pole.

Walking **west**, the bindle arm is therefore the *far* arm, and the frame has
to be re-posed rather than flipped:

- The bindle arm and its gripping hand go **behind** the black fur silhouette.
  The body occludes most of the arm; it does not cross the foreground.
- The pole passes behind the body and over the rear shoulder.
- The bundle trails **behind** the direction of travel — on screen-right — and
  the pole angles up and back toward screen-right. It must never point
  screen-left like a lance.
- The near-side free arm stays fully visible in front of the body, drawn the
  original minimal way: one short thin white curved rubber-hose stroke ending
  in one small circular dot. No glove, fingers, cuff or articulation. It never
  touches the pole and never disappears into the body.
- Pupils look screen-left; the lean, the leading foot and the trailing foot all
  read leftward.

## Character lock

Whoever draws a frame: the identity is fixed. Round dense black shaggy body;
irregular pointed fur silhouette with distinct long head tufts; two large white
oval eyes with black pupils and tiny highlights; thin simple rubber-hose limbs;
minimal black feet; crisp monochrome linework, flat black and white only. No
shading, gradients, grey fills, blur, texture or added detail. Proportions, eye
spacing, body size, fur density, pole length, knot and bundle size all stay put.
Do not make him cleaner, cuter, more dimensional or more anatomically real.

## Frame hygiene

Every frame arrives clean: no dust, smoke, puffs, trailing circles, motion
streaks, ground line or ground shadow, and no label baked into the art. The
area behind the rear foot stays empty. Background transparent.

Between frames, change only what the gait phase needs — bob or lean, pupils,
limb placement, a little fur overlap, restrained pole and bundle follow-through.
Body mass, eye size and separation, pole length and thickness, knot design and
bundle volume all hold steady, and both feet stay on one ground baseline.

## What a west sheet has to match

Measured off `bo-walk.png`'s own export, so a new sheet lands registered without
any nudging:

| | |
|---|---|
| Grid | 6 columns × 6 rows, 36 frames, read left-to-right then down |
| Frame | 554 × 542 px |
| Cadence | 50 ms a frame — 9 frames to a step, 18 to a stride |
| Background | transparent |
| Ink extent | rows 18–530, columns 19–538 of the frame |
| Feet baseline | solid ink ends at row 523, 96.5% down the frame |

The cadence is the part that is easy to miss and impossible to fudge: the band
derives Bo's speed from it, so a sheet at a different frame rate makes his feet
plant in one direction and skate in the other.

## How the west sheet was made

The artwork is supplied, drawn to the brief above — the bindle arm behind the
fur, the bundle trailing screen-right, the free arm in front. It arrived
rendered on a transparency checkerboard rather than with a real alpha channel,
and as 36 independent poses rather than a cycle. Two things were done to it, and
neither touches a line of the drawing:

**Alpha rebuilt.** The checkerboard's two tones (241 and 254) are both lighter
than any ink, so the outline is recoverable directly; filling the regions it
encloses brings back the white the character is *made* of — eyes, the bindle
sack, the pole, the arm — without keeping any checker. Then it is registered to
the east sheet's cell and baseline so the two are interchangeable mid-stride,
and posterized to the same six levels.

**Frames reordered.** In the order supplied, consecutive frames differ almost as
much as frames half a cycle apart — a ratio of **0.92**, which is the signature
of independent samples, and plays as a vibration rather than a walk. Reordering
them into the cyclic sequence that minimises silhouette change between
neighbours brings that to **0.53**, against **0.49** for the east sheet. The
poses did contain a cycle; they were simply shuffled.

**What is not verified: the foot plant.** The east sheet has a clean planted
foot gliding at a measurable −1.33px per frame, which is where the band's whole
speed derivation comes from. The west poses do not carry a consistent ground
contact — the feet-band drift stays noisy in any ordering, with swings of ±12px
— so his feet will not plant against the scrolling ground the way they do going
east. Look at it in the preview before using `facing="west"` in anger. A west
sheet drawn as a true cycle, with the feet on one ground line and the 50ms
cadence, would fix this and drop straight in.

## What the site does to a sheet once it is here

Nothing that changes the drawing. It is repacked to a 6×6 grid at 160×157 per
frame and posterized to six luminance × six alpha levels — invisible at the size
Bo is drawn, and what keeps the sheet under 100KB. The east sheet also had its
bindle sack filled white (it arrived as an outline around transparency, which
vanishes on a dark ground) and its dust puffs removed. A new sheet drawn clean,
per the hygiene rules above, needs neither.

The band derives Bo's pace from the sheet rather than a guess: nine frames to a
step, eighteen to a stride, planted foot tracking back ~11px of a 554px frame
per frame at the export's own 50ms cadence. **A west sheet has to share that
cadence**, or his feet skate in one direction and plant in the other.

## How `bo-stand.png` was made

Bases were taken from the two sheets that are actually Bo — `bo-idle.png`
frame 0 and a narrow-stance frame of `bo-walk.png` (frame 14, picked by
measuring foot spread across all 36 frames rather than by eye). It is not *the*
narrowest: by bottom-band width frame 11 is tighter at 56px against 14's 59px,
and by foot-blob separation ten frames tie. Frame 14 is a legitimately neutral
stance, and the final art derives from the generated stand in any case. The five sheets in `../bo-actions/` were
deliberately **not** used: their character has the bindle on the wrong side, no
feet, and a coarser fur silhouette, so they would have taught the generator the
wrong Bo.

Those two frames went to Higgsfield `nano_banana_2` as image references. The
pose came back on-model; **the blink did not** — the references were strong
enough that "eyes closed" was ignored and the returned blink was byte-similar
to the stand. That turned out to be the right outcome, because a *generated*
blink was the wrong idea anyway: the strip's stated invariant is that alpha is
identical whether his eyes are open or shut, so the outline stays a pure pose
key. A separate drawing cannot guarantee that. The blink is therefore derived
from the stand — eye region cleared, then a wide sliver restored at each eye's
outer corner and a small one at the inner corner, colour channel only — and rest-vs-blink alpha differs by **0 pixels**,
where `bo-idle`'s own two frames differ by 4618.

Two things about the format are easy to get wrong and were, in order:

- **Enclosure, not brightness, separates ink from paper.** `alpha = 255 - luma`
  makes the eye whites and the bindle cloth transparent, because they are as
  white as the page. The paper is flood-filled from the border instead, and
  anything the fill cannot reach is opaque however bright it is.
- **The grey channel is colour; alpha is coverage.** A thin black stroke is
  (grey 0, alpha partial), never (grey 60, alpha 255). Storing raw luma
  conflated them and shipped Bo's legs and feet at grey 41–82 against
  `bo-idle`'s 0 — soft grey sticks instead of ink. Anything within 2px of paper
  is treated as edge and gets coverage; only the interior keeps a real grey, so
  the bindle pole stays light and the legs go nearly black — feet-region mean
  grey 12.9 against the reference's 5.4, down from 41-82, but still 2.4x greyer.
  Mean grey over inked pixels lands at 41.7 against 39.0.

One label to read carefully: the 21726-against-21018 pixel comparison counts
pixels with *any* coverage (`alpha > 0`), a 3.4% gap. Fully opaque pixels
(`alpha == 255`) are 19589 against 18623, a 5.2% gap. Both are fine; the looser
reading is simply the one that got written down first.

He is **not** mirrored, here or anywhere — see above.

The lid slivers are deliberately **asymmetric** — wide at each eye's outer corner,
small at the inner one. An even set of four reads as a dotted line at the size Bo
is actually drawn (roughly 2px a mark in the footer), which is how the first
attempt looked; `bo-idle`'s own blink is lopsided for the same reason.
